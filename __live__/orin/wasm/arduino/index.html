The Arduino's pins

The rig's Arduino (an UNO R4 WiFi) reaches the cart's two limit switches and its motor driver through a few pins. This page shows which pins those are and what each one reads right now, so the wiring can be checked without plugging in a USB cable.

How to read this

High and low. A pin is either high (at the board's 5 V, reported as 1) or low (at 0 V, reported as 0). The Arduino reports the level and nothing more; this page adds the meaning.

How a reading happens. Between readings the board's processor is asleep. Its WiFi chip wakes it with an interrupt (a signal that stops whatever the processor is doing, or ends its sleep) when a message arrives, so it never has to keep checking for one. Every few hundred ms while this page is open, the page asks the Orin, the Orin sends the board one short UDP message (UDP is a way of sending a single message over the network with no connection set up and no guarantee it arrives), and the board reads its pins and sends the levels back.

Inputs and outputs. D2 and D3 are inputs: the level is measured on the pin at that moment. D4, D5 and D6 are outputs: the level is the one the firmware is setting, not a measurement of the wire, so an output can look right with its wire unplugged.

Limit switches. A limit switch sits at each end of the rail and the cart presses it on arrival. Its contact is closed until pressed, which holds the pin low. Pressing it opens the contact and the pin goes high, and so does a broken wire, so a fault looks like a press rather than a safe reading. With a USB cable from the Raspberry Pi plugged into the board, both switches have read high with neither pressed, though not every time (__live__/rpi/rust/crates/arduino-manager/README.md), so be wary of those readings with that cable in.

The motor driver's upside-down signals. Each output reaches the DM542 motor driver through a small transistor, so low switches the driver's input on and high leaves it off. That is why D6 low turns the motor off and D4 pulses low (__documentation__/control-design.md, Why the transistors). Which way the cart goes for Plus and Minus depends on the motor's wiring and is not fixed here.

Power. The board runs from its barrel jack. VIN and the GND beside it are not connected. The jack, 5V and GND are not signals the firmware reads, so they have no live status. If the board answers, it has power, though that power may be coming from a USB cable.

Two separate sides. The Arduino controls only the DM542's 5 V side. The motor side runs on 24 V from its own power supply. The two are kept separate to avoid differing grounds, so no 24 V wire reaches this board.

Not used. D7 was the driver's enable pin before 2026-10-27, and D8 was the old DRV8825 driver's fault line; the DM542 has none. Neither is connected now. The WiFi chip talks to the processor on P501 and P502, which are inside the board and on no header.

Where this comes from. Roles: the pin map in __documentation__/control-design.md. Chip pins: unor4-hal's gpio/pins.rs. Board layout: redrawn and simplified from the top view on Arduino's page for the board. Check any wire against the labels printed on the board itself.
