Calibrate the rail

The stepper motor cannot report where the cart is, so the board has to find out. It runs the cart until it trips a limit switch at one end of the rail, then the other, and counts the motor's pulses between them. From that count it can find the middle of the rail.

Stand at the e-stop before pressing anything. STOP below asks the board over WiFi, and a message can be lost, so it does not replace the e-stop.

How to read this

The order. The board only trusts a switch it saw trip during a move, because a broken wire reads the same as a pressed switch. So it measures the rail's length, its span, only when the cart leaves a switch it saw trip and then trips the other one. From anywhere on the rail that takes two seeks, one to each end, and then the centre is available. If the cart is already resting on a switch after a reset, the board answers already_there and stays put: seek the other end first, then come back.

Why a move takes so long. A seek runs at the firmware's seek_speed, 2,000 pulses per second, which is 30 mm per second. On 2026-11-01 a full rail took 30.5 s and 59,821 pulses, and a move from an end to the centre took 15.2 s and 29,910 pulses. A seek gives up by itself after 34 s.

While the cart moves. The board spends nearly all its time on the motor's pulses. Between pulses it listens for a stop and throws away any other message, so this page stops asking for the switches until the cart has stopped. The moving belt in the drawing only shows that a move is under way and which way it goes. The cart's real position is not known during a move, so it is not drawn at one.

STOP. It only lights up during a move, when the other buttons are off, and they come back once the move has ended. One press sends the board a few messages, because any one of them can be lost on the WiFi. The cart stops in one jump and the motor holds it there, and the move's result says stopped, with how far it got. A stop loses no steps, so the board keeps the span, but the cart is no longer on a switch, so find an end again before moving to the centre.

Where the drawing puts the cart. It only draws the cart at a place the page has a reason for: at an end when that end's switch reads pressed, and at the centre after a centring that worked, with neither switch pressed since. Anywhere else it says it does not know. Moving the cart by hand is invisible to the board and to this page. The span shown is the one this page saw the board measure, and a refreshed page starts knowing nothing, even though the board remembers. The board forgets its span when it resets, when a seek runs out of time, and when a seek trips the wrong switch.

The columns. Status is 200 when the cart did what was asked and 4xx when it did not, with HTTP's meanings: 400 a bad request, 408 out of time, 409 not possible as things stand. 504 means the Orin heard nothing back; the message may have been lost on the WiFi, so the cart may have moved anyway. Result is the board's name for how it ended. Pulses is how far the cart went. The motor's driver turns each pulse into one small step, 0.015 mm of cart travel.

How a move happens. The page asks the Orin's webserver, and the webserver sends the board one UDP message, because a browser cannot send UDP. UDP sends a single message over the network with no connection set up and no guarantee it arrives. The board answers once the cart has stopped. __live__/arduino/rust/crates/calibration/README.md has every answer the board can give.
