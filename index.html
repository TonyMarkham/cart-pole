How to read a frame

  • …so I neededThe caption. In the video, written on the table first, in your voice. It says why the next card had to exist.
  • CardThe card. In the video, drops or flies in after the caption. Title, two or three lines, and a picture.
  • RoadblockA roadblock. The plan hit a wall here, and the card is what got around it.
  • KP 7Key point. The section of project-key-points.md the card comes from.

Frame 0 · the empty blueprint table. Then:

I wanted

A real cart-pole balance bot

Trained entirely in simulation, then dropped onto the real rig. In the video, this card stays pinned at the top of the table from here to the end.

A

The body

Before anything can learn to balance, there has to be a rig to balance on.

01

…so first I needed the robot itself.

KP 1

Mechanical design

The whole rig modelled in FreeCAD, every nut, bolt and washer from the Fasteners Workbench.

Assembly nodes mark where the ArUco markers and MuJoCo sites go.

On card
The FreeCAD assembly, rotated slowly.
Motion
Drops straight down below the want card. The first card on the table.
02

…but FreeCAD's STEP export loses the density.

Roadblock KP 1

STEP density patcher

A Python tool reads each part's density in FreeCAD and writes it into the exported STEP file.

On card
Two lines of STEP text: before without density, after with it.
Motion
Slides in and tucks half under card 01, like a note clipped to it.
03

…and a driver strong enough to move it.

Roadblock KP 15

DM542 driver

The DRV8825 simply could not move the NEMA 23 motor. The DM542 replaced it, at 1/32 microstepping.

On card
Both drivers side by side, the DRV8825 crossed out in red.
Motion
A DRV8825 card lands, then the DM542 drops on top and covers it. The same "replaced" gag returns at beat 13.
B

The digital twin

The same rig, from the same CAD, has to exist in Unity for pictures and in MuJoCo for physics.

04

…I wanted the tools in Rust. But OpenCascade is C++.

Roadblock KP 2

OpenCascade C ABI

occt-dev: build recipes and prebuilt libraries for Windows, Linux and macOS.

A small C ABI in greyhound-snake exposes just the parts needed.

On card
Three stacked layers: C++ · C ABI · Rust and C#.
Motion
Flies in from the left, the first card of a new column.
05

…to turn exact CAD surfaces into meshes.

KP 4

STEP to mesh

A Rust utility that tessellates STEP geometry with OCCT.

On card
The cart as a wireframe, then shaded.
Motion
Drops below card 04.
06

…so Unity could open a STEP file by itself.

KP 5

STEP importer for Unity

Drop a STEP file into Assets and it becomes a GameObject. The STEP file is never touched.

On card
Unity's Project window with the .step asset expanded into its parts.
Motion
Drops below card 05.
07

…and to describe its physics for MuJoCo.

KP 6

MJCF authoring in Unity

Editors for joints, actuators and sites, and one button that exports a MuJoCo-ready MJCF file.

On card
The inspector on one side, the exported XML on the other.
Motion
Drops below card 06, closing the column.
C

The eyes

The robot only knows where the pole is from what its camera sees.

08

…the robot needed eyes.

KP 7

Four ArUco markers

50 mm each: the pole's pivot, the pole's tip, and both ends of the rail.

On card
The four printed markers.
Motion
Flies in from the right, a new column.
09

…but OpenCV couldn't read 50 mm markers from 2 m away.

Roadblock KP 7

Train a detector

A YOLO model learns to find the four markers instead, one class per marker.

On card
A camera frame of the rig from 2 m, markers too small to read.
Motion
An "OpenCV" card lands and gets a red X; the detector card drops beside it.
10

…and a detector needs thousands of labelled pictures.

KP 7

Synthetic image generator

Unity renders the rig on 4 desks in 3 HDRI studios, with random camera, light, cart and pole.

11,000 images: 10,000 to train, 1,000 to check.

On card
A contact sheet of rendered frames, filling in fast.
Motion
A small stack of image cards fans out, then squares up into one card.
11

…and every label had to be exactly right.

KP 7

Exact labels, checked in 8K

Boxes come from Unity's own transforms (WorldToViewportPoint).

An 8K render checks each marker; any the pole hides is dropped.

On card
One frame with its boxes, then a zoom into an 8K crop.
Motion
Drops onto card 10, slightly offset.
12

…and that check needed OpenCV. Also C++.

Roadblock KP 3

OpenCV C ABI

opencv-dev: prebuilt libraries for three platforms, and a small C ABI in greyhound-snake.

On card
The same three layers as card 04, with OpenCV on the bottom.
Motion
Lands next to card 04 in column B, matching it: the pattern repeats.
13

…and the eyes had to be fast.

Roadblock KP 15

Jetson Orin Nano Super

8–10 ms per frame with TensorRT, against 80–125 ms for the best Raspberry Pi 5 options.

On card
The two times as bars, the Pi's ten times longer.
Motion
The beat 03 gag again: a Pi 5 card lands, the Orin drops on top of it.
14

…then I trained it.

KP 7

The eyes work

About 20 hours of training across two models.

Tested on the real rig: a 100% hit rate.

On card
A real camera frame, all four markers boxed.
Motion
Drops at the bottom of column C and the column's cards glow once.
D

The muscles

Something has to turn each decision into the motor's next pulse rate, and it had to be Rust.

15

…an Arduino in Rust. But there was no usable HAL.

Roadblock KP 11

My own HAL

unor4-hal, written from scratch for the UNO R4.

Compile-time hardware validation: `A0` has no GPT output fails at cargo check.

On card
That compiler error, in a terminal.
Motion
Lands hard, with a small shake: the biggest roadblock of the story.
16

…and a HAL needs proof.

KP 11

The R3 test bench

An older UNO R3, running .ino tests, measures the R4's pins.

33 of 33 checks pass, spread over three flashes.

On card
The photo of the R3 and R4 wired together.
Motion
Two small board cards slide in from either side and join into one.
17

…the board had to hear the Orin without polling.

Roadblock KP 12

WiFi bridge fork

Push mode: each UDP datagram goes to the processor as it arrives, and an interrupt wakes it.

On card
Two timelines: polling's constant checks, push's single wake-up.
Motion
Drops below card 16.
18

…then the firmware itself.

KP 13

The controller

A 3-byte command over WiFi; it sleeps until an interrupt wakes it.

Limit switches, a soft limit and FAULT keep the cart on the rail.

On card
The UNO R4 on the rig, with the DM542 beside it.
Motion
Drops below card 17.
19

…and I needed to flash it from my desk.

KP 14

Arduino manager

A Raspberry Pi beside the rig logs the board's USB serial line and flashes new firmware on request.

On card
The manager's web page with the live serial log.
Motion
Drops at the bottom of column D.
E

The clock

The real robot is never instant. Training has to include its delays, so they had to be measured first.

20

…the policy had to feel the real delays.

KP 8

Timing measurements

Seven gaps measured on the real loop, from the camera's exposure to the motor's new rate.

Training draws four random stretches from them, and loses 1 command in 3,000.

On card
The loop as a ring, each stretch a coloured arc with its range in ms.
Motion
Lands in the middle of the table, where threads C and D meet: it measures both.
F

The learning

Everything above comes together in simulation, where the policy practises millions of times.

21

…it needed to know what "good" looks like.

KP 9

The reward

DeepMind's dm_control cart-pole reward: upright × small control × small velocity × centred.

On card
The four factors as four dials, multiplied.
Motion
Drops alone at the right edge, a new column.
22

…and somewhere to practise.

KP 10

The synthetic loop

MuJoCo, the Unity camera and stand-ins for the Orin and Arduino, 12 at once. It sees only detected markers.

6,000,000 steps in about 19.5 hours.

On card
The 3 × 4 grid of training windows, all moving.
Motion
Thin lines draw from columns B, C, D and E into this card before it lands: it uses all of them.
G

The real thing

The trained policy leaves the simulator and goes onto the robot.

23

…and a brain on the real robot.

KP 15

The brain

The same loop, in Rust on the Orin. Camera calibrated to 0.20 px.

Run and STOP from a page in Rust compiled to WebAssembly.

On card
The /run page with its Run and STOP buttons.
Motion
Drops at the bottom centre, under everything else.
24

…and then it balanced.

KP 15 · 2026-11-09

It balances

On its first day on the rig, the 6M-step policy swung the pole up in 3.4 s and held it within 10° of upright for the rest of a 30 s run.

On card
The video of the real rig balancing, playing inside the card.
Motion
Every other card dims. This one drops last, dead centre. Then the want card at the top gets a red BUILT stamp.

The last frame

The camera pulls back to show the whole table: the want pinned at the top, seven columns of cards beneath it, and the balancing rig at the bottom.
